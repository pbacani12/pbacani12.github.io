Reading and Written Response #1: "In Plato's Cave"

Photography is a universal practice nowadays, and with the constant progression of our cell phones, it has become much easier to participate in this art form. However, some people have different purposes for using their camera. Some take pictures with the intent to share it on social media, while others take them with the intent to send it to family members to let them know that they are okay. There are a plethora of reasons why people take pictures, and I find it fascinating how present photography is without realizing.
Personally, I take pictures often. Despite being the person who takes the least photos in my family, I still think I take more pictures than the average joe. There’s just this indescribable feeling of showing someone a picture and hearing them say, “This picture is fire!” or “Woah you took that??”. I was recently gifted this mini digital camera and I’ve been obsessed with taking pictures on it. Bringing it to family events and capturing everyone’s reaction to how small and functional it is brings me so much joy and I love how the pictures come out afterwards (for reference, the dimensions of the camera are 1.5 x 1 x 0.5 inches). There is this function on the iPhone called Live Photos that captures a picture but also holds a small video of before and after the picture was taken. Many people have it on but I choose to keep it off for two reasons. 1. Because it takes up more storage (For some reason I didn’t upgrade my phone to the highest amount of storage.) and 2. Because I think of taking Live pictures as an act of cheating. Granted, if I had the storage, I would be taking live pictures as much as the next person. But having this restriction reminded me of how amazing it feels to take a perfect picture without the ability to “change the cover” of a live image. Being there at the perfect time and pressing the button at the perfect moment and having the picture come out the way you imagined is such an amazing feeling.
Although many fantastic pictures are a result of the joyous intent to simply want to capture life and make art, some people have not-so positive reasons for taking images. Some people take pictures for the sole purpose of posting on social media. Many people, like myself, post on social media to gain validation and/or recognition from peers or even strangers. Even though social media has given people a platform to show their creations and gain many opportunities, people can become obsessed with posting to the point where it consumes an individual entirely. The once innocent purpose of capturing images is taken over by the need to get a certain amount of likes in order to gain enough money to sustain one’s self. Their creations, once coming from a feeling of passion, are now turned into emotionless pieces of “hard work”.
Photographs are especially important now. It can spark hope and determination in a world filled with hate and sorrow. It can emphasize movements and educate the uneducated. Photographs are taken to show the truth in a world full of lies.
