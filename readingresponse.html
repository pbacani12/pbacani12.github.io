Reading and Written Response #1: "In Plato's Cave"

Photography is a universal practice nowadays, and with the constant progression of our cell phones, it has become much easier to participate in this art form. However, some people have different purposes for using their camera. Some take pictures with the intent to share it on social media, while others take them with the intent to send it to family members to let them know that they are okay. There are a plethora of reasons why people take pictures, and I find it fascinating how present photography is without realizing. Personally, I take pictures often. Despite being the person who takes the least photos in my family, I still think I take more pictures than the average joe. There’s just this indescribable feeling of showing someone a picture and hearing them say, “This picture is fire!” or “Woah you took that??”. I was recently gifted this mini digital camera and I’ve been obsessed with taking pictures on it. Bringing it to family events and capturing everyone’s reaction to how small and functional it is brings me so much joy and I love how the pictures come out afterwards (for reference, the dimensions of the camera are 1.5 x 1 x 0.5 inches). There is this function on the iPhone called Live Photos that captures a picture but also holds a small video of before and after the picture was taken. Many people have it on but I choose to keep it off for two reasons. 1. Because it takes up more storage (For some reason I didn’t upgrade my phone to the highest amount of storage.) and 2. Because I think of taking Live pictures as an act of cheating. Granted, if I had the storage, I would be taking live pictures as much as the next person. But having this restriction reminded me of how amazing it feels to take a perfect picture without the ability to “change the cover” of a live image. Being there at the perfect time and pressing the button at the perfect moment and having the picture come out the way you imagined is such an amazing feeling. Although many fantastic pictures are a result of the joyous intent to simply want to capture life and make art, some people have not-so positive reasons for taking images. Some people take pictures for the sole purpose of posting on social media. Many people, like myself, post on social media to gain validation and/or recognition from peers or even strangers. Even though social media has given people a platform to show their creations and gain many opportunities, people can become obsessed with posting to the point where it consumes an individual entirely. The once innocent purpose of capturing images is taken over by the need to get a certain amount of likes in order to gain enough money to sustain one’s self. Their creations, once coming from a feeling of passion, are now turned into emotionless pieces of “hard work”. Photographs are especially important now. It can spark hope and determination in a world filled with hate and sorrow. It can emphasize movements and educate the uneducated. Photographs are taken to show the truth in a world full of lies.

Reading and Written Response #2: "'AI slop' distorting our reality, the world is sleepwalking into disaster"

I hate AI slop. I try not to use the word hate a lot, but this is something that I think should not exist. I remember during the early 2020s where one of the first AI generated images started popping up on social media. People were making fun of how bad that video of Will Smith eating spaghetti was and people went on with their day. In less than a decade, generative AI images and videos have rapidly improved. There was a video comparing the old Will Smith spaghetti AI video with a 2025 version using the same prompt and the difference is extremely disturbing. Nowadays, AI slop is everywhere. Whether it’s security camera footage of rabbits jumping on a trampoline or someone’s orange palette profile picture, it’s starting to become much harder to differentiate what is real and what isn’t. I’m an artist and I have many friends who are artists as well. I’ve been seeing many mutuals complain about how AI is making it harder for them to find a job (as if it wasn’t already hard enough competing with other talented human beings). It’s extremely upsetting seeing these people not be able to pursue their dreams because of the greed corporations have. They think, “Instead of hiring unemployed talented artists, we should just ask AI to animate the next CocaCola ad because it’s more efficient!” The ad looked horrible!!!! It literally looked like SLOP. I have no idea why people would prefer AI slop over human-made animations and illustrations. You can see how “hollow” AI generated images are and this “shortcut” isn’t even a shortcut sometimes. When you generate images and it doesn’t look exactly like what you had imagined it, you have to go on this constant back and forth and even then the machine won’t even give you what you want. One of the most disgusting and terrifying things about the improvement of AI is how someone can easily make an AI image of you (if they have a reference photo). Grok, X’s AI bot, has been given prompts from disgusting people to take the clothes off of women and little girls whose pictures happen to be on the platform. It’s absolutely insulting and infuriating that these people who are typing these prompts aren’t being arrested or accounted for this crime of sexual harassment. People also ask Grok to take other people’s art and change up or add something just for the fun of it. It’s not only images! Now, I see videos warning others about scammers using a loved one’s voice in order to kidnap or manipulate someone. The world is starting to become an even more terrifying place to be a part of. Not to mention, this is doing harmful things to our environment. Houses that used to be in nice suburban areas are now being torn down to make room for more AI industries. People who live next to these datacenters have no access to fresh drinking water. Picking up a pencil does not harm anyone. Exercising your creative muscles benefits an individual. And honestly, it’s cooler to tell someone “I made that” instead of “I asked ChatGPT to make that.”

Reading and Written Response #3: “Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis”

Reading through these stories made me extremely depressed. The way the stories were portrayed through visuals really emphasized the struggle of unstable healthcare in Sierra Leone. The realistic art style did an amazing job in capturing the emotions of the four women. I think without the visuals, the story wouldn’t really have the same effect. Nowadays, I feel as though people are desensitized to major life-changing events. When stories like these are portrayed through only text, people may just read it and not really be able to immerse themselves and understand. The art puts people directly into the scenario and forces them to witness this kind of reality. This way, it pushes people to become more aware of the importance of healthcare. Many individuals take things for granted, and I feel these stories remind people to be grateful that healthcare is really accessible. It is a slippery slope, however, due to how hard it is to book an appointment for a specific doctor. This has actually happened to me once. I tried to book an appointment, but had to find out the next available appointment wouldn’t be until 6 months after. It was pretty ridiculous and since then, we haven’t thought about going back (primarily because I don’t have to go that often anymore but the lack of appointments is part of why we don’t go). While I was reading these stories, it kind of freaked me out. As a woman-bodied individual, hearing about horror stories of childbirth and pregnancy has always scared me. My relationship with my body is really important to me. I’m grateful that said relationship is pretty healthy compared to my peers and other individuals. Hearing about the way pregnancy alters your body forever shakes me up in a way that’s really hard to explain. The woman's body is something that is extremely fascinating. But due to many uneducated people, they try to control what people can and can’t do with their bodies. Heela’s story reminded me of the struggles of abortion. When you hear the debate about abortion, you may automatically think about the “Pro-life vs Pro-choice” argument. While that topic is important, people forget that some women just cannot bear children. People focus on the child but don’t stop and think about the health of the one carrying said child. Forcing someone to give birth to their baby contradicts “Pro-lifers” point of view because they disregard the idea that the mother (and/or child) could die after childbirth. It irritates me to think that people (mainly men) can decide what we can and can’t do with our bodies. Why do they think they have the authority to criticize and control our women’s bodies?
